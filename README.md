# Regional Water Use Efficiency in California Vineyards
***
**Author:** William Mullins | **Date:** 12-12-2025

## About  

California's agriculture is the state's dominant water consumer, accounting for approximately 80% of its developed water supply. The efficiency of this water use is paramount for economic resilience and environmental stewardship, particularly as climate change exacerbates drought frequency and intensifies competition for limited resources.

Vineyards represent a significant and geographically diverse component of the state's agricultural portfolio, spanning disparate climate zones from the cool coastal valleys to the hot interior regions. While climatic factors are known drivers of water demand, regional differences inherent to California's 10 major hydrologic regions include additional variables such as such as water management practices, regulatory structures (e.g., local Groundwater Sustainability Agencies), soil characteristics, and crop varieties—may exert an independent and measurable influence on observed water use efficiency.

The aim of this analysis is to quantify the magnitude and direction of the independent effect of hydrologic region on vineyard water use efficiency across California.

## Data  This analysis utilizes two key datasets:

The analysis uses the Statewide Agricultural Water Use Dataset (2016-2020) from the California Department of Water Resources. This comprehensive dataset contains annual water use information for agricultural operations across all regions at the county, hydrologic, and statewide level.

Data availability: [California DWR Water Use Data](https://data.ca.gov/dataset/statewide-agricultural-water-use-data-2016-2020)
